\title{The unrestricted optimum after Conjecture 20:\\ the one-dimensional mechanism, a width theorem,\\ and what can and cannot be proved}

\begin{abstract}
We tried to prove that the unrestricted maximum margin equals the paper's prediction, either the interference-free (IF) value
$\nu^{1-s}B_p\Gamma_s(p)$ or the price--programme value $\pi_{n,b}(p)B_p\Gamma_s(p)$ of the companion note.  The outcome is
as follows.
(1) A universal proof of the IF value is impossible.  At $p=3$, $b=2$ we identify the mechanism behind the counterexamples
exactly: a \emph{shared-sign, one-dimensional} family whose feature field is a rotating phase times a real exponential sum.
Searching this family (exact LP for the weights, outer search over $m$ real slopes) gives new certified networks with
$\gamma/\gamma_{\rm IF}=1.063,\,1.193,\,1.184,\,1.457,\,1.617,\,1.595,\,2.011,\,2.212$ for $n=11,\dots,18$.  The excess grows with $n$.
(2) We prove a \emph{width theorem}: a slot-symmetric single-feature network whose input vectors share a sign pattern needs
at least $\nu-\lfloor\nu/p\rfloor$ neurons.  Every network in (1) attains this bound.  The saving that drives the
counterexamples is therefore at most a $1/p$ fraction of the neurons, and is zero when $p>\nu$, which covers the paper's
experimental range.
(3) For $p=5,7$ the same mechanism, run at its minimal width, loses to IF by a factor that grows with $n$ (efficiency
$\le0.19$ for $p=5$, $n\le16$, and decreasing).  Releasing it into the full slot-symmetric class did not help at the point we tested ($n=10$).
(4) Decoupling, the remaining step of the price--programme conjecture, is \emph{not} automatic: at $p=5$ a mean-zero
frequency-2 field carried by the same neurons raises a feature's margin by factors $1.2$--$2.6$.  In every case we found
it still stays $\ge4\times$ below IF.
So the correct universal statement is the price--programme form, and the open content is concentrated in two precise
questions for $p\ge5$.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setting and notation}
As in the paper and the companion note: $p$ odd prime, $\rho=e^{2\pi i/p}$, $\theta:=\pi/p$, $\nu=n+1$, $s=\nu/b$.  A
\emph{single-feature} network has output vectors at frequency $1$ only; its margin is $h=\kappa_{\min}(p)\min_x\mathrm{cm}(\Phi(x))$,
where $\Phi(x)=\sum_iW_i\prod_tu_{it}(x_t)\rho^{x_t}$ and $\mathrm{cm}(z)=\Re z-\cot\theta\,|\Im z|$ (companion note,
Lemmas 1--2).  So $h>0$ iff every value $\Phi(x)$ lies in the open sector $|\arg z|<\theta$.  A network is
\emph{slot-symmetric} if $u_{it}=u_i$ for all $t$; then $\Phi$ depends on $x$ only through the count vector
$N\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^p$, $N_a=\#\{t:x_t=a\}$:
\[\Phi(N)=\sum_{i=1}^mW_i\prod_{a\in\mathbb Z_p}\big(u_i(a)\rho^a\big)^{N_a}.\]
\emph{Efficiency} means $\gamma/\gamma_{\rm IF}$ at $b=2$ (for single-feature networks at $p\ge5$, $n\ge3$ this is the same as
the ratio of the single-feature margin to the IF single-feature margin, because $K^*(s,p)=1$).

\section{Shared-sign networks and their fields}
\begin{definition}
A slot-symmetric network has a \emph{shared sign pattern} $\varepsilon\in\{\pm1\}^{p}$ if $\varepsilon_au_i(a)\ge0$ for all
$i,a$.  The \emph{sine pattern} is $\varepsilon_a=+1$ for $0\le a<p/2$ and $-1$ for $p/2<a<p$, with exponents
$e_a:=2a$ resp.\ $2a-p$, a permutation of $\{0,\dots,p-1\}$ satisfying $\varepsilon_a\rho^a=e^{i\theta e_a}$.  The
\emph{one-dimensional (1-D) family} consists of the sine-pattern networks with $u_i(a)=\varepsilon_a e^{\lambda_ie_a}$
(up to scale), $\lambda_i\in\mathbb R$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[fields]\label{lem:field}
For a sine-pattern network, $\Phi(N)=e^{i\theta\tau(N)}S(N)$ with $\tau(N)=\sum_ae_aN_a\in\{0,\dots,(p-1)n\}$ and
$S(N)=\sum_iW_i\prod_a|u_i(a)|^{N_a}$, an exponential sum with positive bases.  In the 1-D family
\[\Phi(N)=e^{i\theta\tau}\sum_{i=1}^mV_ie^{\lambda_i\tau},\qquad\tau=\tau(N),\]
so the margin condition is a condition on the $L+1$ values $\tau=0,\dots,L$, $L=(p-1)n$, of a rotating phase times a
real-exponential sum.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
$u_i(a)\rho^a=|u_i(a)|\varepsilon_a\rho^a=|u_i(a)|e^{i\theta e_a}$; take the product over $a$ with exponents $N_a$.
\end{proof}

At $p=3$ the certified counterexample of the earlier notes ($n=11$, width $8$) is, to three digits, a 1-D network: all
eight input vectors have the sign pattern $(-,+,+)$ (a translate of the sine pattern), and
$\log|u_i(a)|-\log|u_i(0)|=\lambda_i\cdot(0,-1,1)$ with ratio $-1.00\pm0.03$.  Re-optimising it inside the 1-D family
raises its efficiency from $1.0544$ to $1.0631$.

\section{A width theorem}
\begin{theorem}[width]\label{thm:width}
Let $p$ be an odd prime, $n\ge1$, and let a slot-symmetric single-feature network with a shared sign pattern have
positive margin.  Then its width is
\[m\ \ge\ \nu-\Big\lfloor\frac{\nu}{p}\Big\rfloor .\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Positive margin is an open condition, so we may perturb zero entries of the $u_i$ to small values of the prescribed sign.
Hence assume all $u_i(a)\ne0$.  Put $d=(p-1)/2$.  The map $a\mapsto a+d$ is a cyclic permutation of $\mathbb Z_p$ of odd
length, so $\varepsilon$ cannot alternate along it, and there is a symbol $b$ with $\varepsilon_b=\varepsilon_{b+d}$.  Let
$a=b+d$ and consider the $n+1$ count vectors $N(k)=(n-k)\mathbf e_b+k\mathbf e_a$, $k=0,\dots,n$.  With
$r_i=u_i(a)/u_i(b)>0$,
\[\Phi(N(k))=C\,\big(e^{i(\pi-\theta)}\big)^k\,S(k),\qquad S(k)=\sum_ic_ir_i^k ,\]
for constants $C\in\mathbb C\setminus\{0\}$, $c_i\in\mathbb C$, because $\rho^{d}=e^{i(\pi-\theta)}$.  Positive margin gives
$\arg S(k)\in I_k:=(\omega_k-\theta,\omega_k+\theta)$ with $\omega_k=-\arg C-(\pi-\theta)k$.  Note
$\omega_k\equiv-\arg C+\theta k\pmod\pi$.

For $\psi\in\mathbb R$ let $h_\psi(k)=\Im(e^{-i\psi}S(k))=\sum_i\Im(e^{-i\psi}c_i)\,r_i^k$.  After merging equal bases this is
a real exponential sum with at most $m$ terms.  By Laguerre's rule of signs it has at most $m-1$ real zeros unless it
vanishes identically, so the sequence $h_\psi(0),\dots,h_\psi(n)$ has at most $m-1$ sign changes.  Fix a residue $k_0$ and
take $\psi=-\arg C+\theta k_0$.  The arc $I_k$ meets the line $\psi+\pi\mathbb Z$ iff
$\theta(k-k_0)\in(-\theta,\theta)+\pi\mathbb Z$, i.e.\ iff $k\equiv k_0\pmod p$.  Call these $k$ \emph{undetermined}.  For
every other $k$ the sign of $h_\psi(k)$ is fixed by the side of the line on which $I_k$ lies.
\begin{itemize}
\item If $k,k+1$ are both determined, $I_{k+1}=I_k-\pi+\theta$; an arc of length $2\theta$ on one side, rotated by
$-\pi+\theta$ and still avoiding the line, lies on the other side, so the signs differ.
\item If $k$ is undetermined and $0<k<n$, then $k\pm1$ are determined ($p\ge3$), and with $I_k$ centred on the line,
$I_{k-1}$ and $I_{k+1}$ are the arcs of length $2\theta$ immediately on either side of it, so $h_\psi(k-1)$ and
$h_\psi(k+1)$ again have opposite signs.
\end{itemize}
So the determined values alternate in sign and there are $|D|-1$ sign changes, where $D$ is the set of determined $k$.
$h_\psi\not\equiv0$, since otherwise every $S(k)$ would lie on the line, contradicting $D\ne\emptyset$.  Choosing $k_0$ in a
least populated residue class of $\{0,\dots,n\}$ gives $|D|=n+1-\lfloor\nu/p\rfloor$, hence
$m-1\ge n-\lfloor\nu/p\rfloor$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:width}
(i) For $p>\nu$ a shared-sign single-feature network needs at least $\nu$ neurons, as many as the IF block.
(ii) For every $p$, the width saving is at most $\lfloor\nu/p\rfloor$.  For networks with equal amplitudes at $b=2$, the
width factor $(\nu/m)^{s-1}$ in the cost comparison with the IF block is therefore at most
$\big(\nu/(\nu-\lfloor\nu/p\rfloor)\big)^{s-1}\le\big(p/(p-1)\big)^{s-1}$: up to $(3/2)^{s-1}$ at $p=3$, but at most
$(5/4)^{s-1}$ at $p=5$.
(iii) Every certified counterexample in Table~\ref{tab:p3} attains the bound with equality.
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}
The paper's experiments use $\nu\le5$ and $p\ge11$, so by (i) the mechanism behind the counterexamples has no width
advantage anywhere in that range.  Width is not the only lever: per unit cost, a flat sign vector has $2^{n/2}$ times
the raw output of a cosine input.  But numerically this lever alone never sufficed (Section~\ref{sec:p5}).
\end{remark}

\section{The $p=3$ optimum: reduction and certified values}
\paragraph{Reduction.}  In the 1-D family, for fixed slopes $\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_m$ the cone constraints at
$\tau=0,\dots,L$ are linear in $(\Re V_i,\Im V_i)$, and the cost amplitude of neuron $i$ is
$\sqrt{p/2}\,|V_i|\,Z(\lambda_i)^n$ with $Z(\lambda)^2=\sum_{k<p}e^{2\lambda k}$.  Maximising the margin under a weighted
$\ell_1$ budget is therefore a second-order cone programme (an LP after polygonal approximation of $|V_i|$).  The concave
$\ell_{2/\nu}$ cost is handled by reweighting; in practice the optimal amplitudes are nearly equal and uniform weights
suffice.  The whole problem reduces to choosing $m$ real slopes.  We search them with CMA-ES and continuation in $n$.
All values below are then re-verified independently.

\paragraph{Structure of the optimum.}  Each term sits at the cost-optimal tilt for the stretch of $\tau$ it covers.  The
cost amplitudes agree to within $\sim10\%$, consecutive terms have nearly antipodal phases ($\approx172^\circ$ apart at
$n=11$), and the margin is active at most $\tau$.  The field is a $10^{-3}$--$10^{-4}$ residue of terms up to $10^3$ times
larger, kept inside the $60^\circ$ sector at every count class.

\section{$p\ge5$: the mechanism loses}\label{sec:p5}
\paragraph{Gain/loss accounting.}  For a 1-D network with nearly equal amplitudes,
$\gamma/\gamma_{\rm IF}=\zeta\cdot2^{n/2}\cdot(\nu/m)^{s-1}$.  Here $2^{n/2}$ is the raw advantage of flat sign vectors
over cosines, $(\nu/m)^{s-1}$ the width saving (capped by Theorem~\ref{thm:width}), and $\zeta$ the fraction of the
mid-range envelope that survives cancellation.  The available gain per unit of $n$ is
$\tfrac12\log\frac{2p}{p-1}$: $0.55$ at $p=3$, $0.46$ at $p=5$, $0.42$ at $p=7$.  Numerically, $\log\zeta/n\approx-0.48$ at $p=3$ ($n=18$) and
$\approx-0.575$ at $p=5$ (stable over $n=13$--$17$).  That leaves net rates of about $+0.07$ and $-0.12$ per unit of $n$,
consistent with the observed growth at $p=3$ and decline at $p=5$.

\section{Cross-feature compensation: decoupling is not automatic}
At $p\ge5$ several frequencies coexist, and the margin condition at $x$ is that the vector $(\Phi_l(x))_l$ lies in a
polyhedron, not that each $\Phi_l(x)$ lies in its own sector.  Concretely, at $p=5$ a frequency-2 value
$\Phi_2\approx0.25|\Phi_1|e^{-i18^\circ}$ makes $\Phi_1$ admissible at $\arg\Phi_1=60^\circ$, the $p=3$ sector.
The same neurons can produce such a $\Phi_2$ almost for free: the field is a $10^{-3}$ residue of much larger terms, so a
frequency-2 output component with $\sim10^{-3}$ relative weight, costing $\sim10^{-6}$ of the budget, gives a $\Phi_2$ of the
right size.  It is limited only by geometry: its phase follows a different linear functional of the counts.

We tested this with an exact LP over output vectors spanning frequencies $1$ and $2$, at the slopes of the best 1-D networks,
on all count classes and all four shifts $\delta$.  Measured gains over frequency-1 outputs at the same neurons: $\times1.17$
($n=8$), $\times1.36$ ($n=12$), up to $\times2.6$ at non-optimal widths.  With a CMA search over slopes for the joint problem:
$0.197$ ($n=8$) and $0.173$ ($n=12$).  The frequency-2 output carries $0.2$--$3\%$ of the output energy.  So the decoupling half of
the price--programme conjecture (companion note, Conjecture~20$'$) cannot be proved feature by feature: one feature can live outside its own sector where
another, interference-only, field compensates.  In every configuration we tried, the compensated feature still stays
$\ge4\times$ below IF.

\section{The number of features of the unrestricted optimum}\label{sec:count}
The paper's target is the \emph{count}: a frequency $j$ is a feature of $\theta$ if $A_j(\theta)=|\beta_j(\theta)|>0$, and the
prediction is that every globally margin-optimal network uses $K^*(s,p)=|\operatorname{spt}y^\star|$ features, with
amplitudes proportional to a maximiser $y^\star$ of the programme $\Gamma_s(p)$.  This is weaker than the value prediction,
and it survives the counterexamples.

\begin{proposition}[what is unconditional]\label{prop:count}
Let $\theta^\star$ be a global maximiser of the $L_{2,b}$-margin.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] If $p=3$, then $\theta^\star$ uses exactly $K^*(s,3)=1$ feature, for every $n$ and $b$.
\item[(b)] If $\theta^\star$ is interference-free, its amplitudes are proportional to a maximiser of $\Gamma_s(p)$, so it uses
exactly $|\operatorname{spt}y^\star|$ features (paper, Theorem 8(1)).
\item[(c)] If $b_0\le b\le\nu$ (interval of the earlier notes, Part II), then $\theta^\star$ is interference-free and uses all
$F=K^*(s,p)$ frequencies with equal amplitudes.
\item[(d)] For every $p,n,b$: write $\eta(\theta):=\|A(\theta)\|_{1/s}/(B_p\|\theta\|_{2,b}^\nu)\le1$ for the clean efficiency
and $y=A/\|A\|_{1/s}$ for the clean allocation of $\theta^\star$.  Then $\eta(\theta^\star)\ge\pi_{n,b}(p)$ and
$\min_{\delta\ne0}\sum_jy_j\kappa_j(\delta)\ge\frac{\pi_{n,b}(p)}{\eta(\theta^\star)}\,\Gamma_s(p)$.  In particular, if
$\eta(\theta^\star)=\pi_{n,b}(p)$, then $y$ is a programme maximiser and $\theta^\star$ uses exactly $|\operatorname{spt}y|$ features.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
(a) $F=\{1\}$, and $A_1>0$ by the paper's Lemma 2 because the margin is positive.  (b), (c) are the cited results.
(d) By the price--programme theorem of the companion note,
$h(\theta^\star)=\gamma^*_b\|\theta^\star\|^\nu\ge\pi B_p\Gamma_s\|\theta^\star\|^\nu$.
By the paper's Lemma 2, $h\le\min_\delta\sum_jA_j\kappa_j(\delta)=\|A\|_{1/s}\min_\delta\sum_jy_j\kappa_j(\delta)\le\|A\|_{1/s}\Gamma_s$.
Divide by $B_p\|\theta^\star\|^\nu$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[decoupling suffices; the price is irrelevant]\label{prop:dec}
Suppose a global maximiser $\theta^\star$ satisfies the decoupling inequality
\[h(\theta^\star)\ \le\ \pi_{n,b}(p)\,B_p\,\min_{\delta\ne0}\sum_{j}P_j^{\,s}\kappa_j(\delta)\qquad\text{for some }P_j\ge0,\ \ \sum_jP_j\le\|\theta^\star\|_{2,b}^b .\]
Then $(P_j^{\,s}/\|\theta^\star\|^{\nu}_{2,b})_j$ is a maximiser of $\Gamma_s(p)$.  If moreover the features of $\theta^\star$ are exactly
the frequencies it pays for ($A_j>0\iff P_j>0$), then $\theta^\star$ uses exactly $K^*$ features.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
With $z_j=P_j^s/\|\theta^\star\|^\nu$ we have $\sum_jz_j^{1/s}\le1$, and
\[\pi B_p\Gamma_s\|\theta^\star\|^\nu\le h(\theta^\star)\le\pi B_p\|\theta^\star\|^\nu\min_\delta\sum_jz_j\kappa_j(\delta).\]
Hence $z$ attains $\Gamma_s$.
\end{proof}
The price enters only as a common factor, because relabelling $a\mapsto ja$ makes it the same for every frequency and scaling
makes it homogeneous of degree $s$ in the budget.  So the count needs \emph{only} decoupling.  Neither $\pi=\nu^{1-s}$ nor
interference-freeness is needed.

\begin{theorem}[exact count in the dense regime; lower bound in general]\label{thm:dense}
For $S\subseteq F$ let $\Gamma_s^{(S)}(p)$ be the programme restricted to allocations supported on $S$, and
$K_{\rm low}(s,p,\nu):=\min\{|S|:\Gamma^{(S)}_s(p)\ge\nu^{1-s}\Gamma_s(p)\}$.  Every global maximiser of the $L_{2,b}$-margin,
of any width and with interference allowed, uses at least $K_{\rm low}$ features.  In particular, if
\[\nu^{s-1}\,\Gamma_s^{(F\setminus\{j\})}(p)<\Gamma_s(p)\qquad(\text{independent of }j\text{ by relabelling}),\]
every global maximiser uses all $F=(p-1)/2$ frequencies.  This is the exact count $K^*(s,p)=F$ in the dense regime.  The
condition holds for $s\le1.331,1.285,1.257$ ($p=5$; $\nu=3,4,5$), $s\le1.154,1.130,1.115$ ($p=7$), $s\le1.079,1.066,1.058$
($p=11$), $s\le1.064,1.053,1.047$ ($p=13$).  In every case computed this is $2.5$ to $5$ times the range $s-1\le1/(F(2\nu+1)-2)$ of Part II.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
If the features of $\theta^\star$ lie in $S$, Proposition 3 of the paper restricted to $S$ and the single-neuron budget give
$\gamma(\theta^\star)\le B_p\Gamma_s^{(S)}$, while $\gamma(\theta^\star)=\gamma^*_b\ge\nu^{1-s}B_p\Gamma_s$ (Theorem 8).  Relabelling
$a\mapsto ka$ maps allocations on $S$ to allocations on $kS$ with the same value, and acts transitively on $F$.
\end{proof}
\paragraph{Why the upper bound is the hard half.}  Let $q$ be KKT multipliers of a maximiser on its active constraints
$(x,\delta)$.  Stationarity in the output vector makes neuron $i$'s frequency-$j$ output proportional to
$\sum_xg_i(x)\rho^{-jy(x)}\overline{Q_j(x)}$, with $g_i(x)=\prod_tu_{it}(x_t)$ and $Q_j(x)=\sum_\delta q(x,\delta)(1-\rho^{-j\delta})$.
If $q$ is uniform in $x$, as for any interference-free maximiser (average $q$ over translations), this gives
$\beta_{ij}\propto Q_j\,|\sum_xg_i(x)\rho^{-jy(x)}|^2$ with $\Re Q_j=\sum_\delta q(\delta)\kappa_j(\delta)>0$.  Then every frequency at which
some neuron's input product has a non-zero clean projection is a feature.  So ``no more than $K^*$ features'' is
equivalent to frequency purity of the maximiser's input products at the non-programme frequencies.  Interference-free
maximisers have it (paper, Theorem 8(2)).  A maximiser built from sign-vector inputs, like the $p=3$ networks, would
generically carry every frequency.  The upper bound is therefore not separable from the question of which
input structure is optimal at $p\ge5$.

\begin{theorem}[the count is $\Theta(\log p)$ for every near-optimal network]\label{thm:theta}
Let $2\le b<\nu$, $s=\nu/b$, $r=1/s$, and for a non-zero amplitude vector $A$ let
\[K_r(A):=\frac{\big(\sum_jA_j^{\,r}\big)^{1/(1-r)}}{\big(\sum_jA_j\big)^{r/(1-r)}}\ \le\ |\operatorname{spt}A| ,\]
the participation number of $A$ (it equals $K$ for $K$ equal non-zero entries).  Let $\theta$ be any product network,
of any width and with interference allowed, with $\gamma(\theta)\ge\lambda\,\nu^{1-s}B_p\Gamma_s(p)$ for some $\lambda\in(0,1]$.
Every global maximiser has $\lambda\ge\pi_{n,b}(p)\nu^{s-1}\ge1$.  Then
\[K_r(A(\theta))\ \le\ \nu\Big(\frac{p}{(p-1)\lambda\,\Gamma_s(p)}\Big)^{\frac1{s-1}}\ \le\ \nu\Big(\frac{2p}{(p-1)\lambda}\Big)^{\frac1{s-1}}\lceil8\ln p\rceil .\]
Moreover, for every $M\ge1$ the $M$ largest amplitudes carry at least a fraction $1-(K_r/M)^{1-r}$ of $\sum_jA_j$.
Together with the paper's Corollary~12, which shows that the support of such a network has at least $c_{\nu,b,\lambda}\ln p$
frequencies, the feature count of every margin-optimal network is $\Theta_{\nu,b}(\log p)$.  This is the order of
$K^*(s,p)$ (paper, Theorem~11), for every odd prime, unconditionally.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Averaging the paper's Lemma 2 over $\delta\ne0$ and using $\sum_{\delta\ne0}\kappa_j(\delta)=p$ gives
$h\le\frac{p}{p-1}\sum_jA_j$.  The single-neuron budget gives $\sum_jA_j^r\le\sum_{i,j}|\beta_{ij}|^r\le C_bP$ with
$P=\|\theta\|_{2,b}^b$.  Hence, with $h=\gamma P^s$ and $sr=1$,
\[K_r(A)\le\frac{(C_bP)^{1/(1-r)}}{\big(\frac{p-1}p\gamma P^s\big)^{r/(1-r)}}
=\Big(\frac{p\,C_b^{1/r}}{(p-1)\gamma}\Big)^{\frac r{1-r}}=\Big(\frac{p\,B_p}{(p-1)\gamma}\Big)^{\frac1{s-1}},\]
because $C_b^{1/r}=C_b^{\nu/b}=B_p$ and $r/(1-r)=1/(s-1)$.  Insert $\gamma\ge\lambda\nu^{1-s}B_p\Gamma_s$, then
$\Gamma_s\ge\frac12\lceil8\ln p\rceil^{1-s}$ (paper, Theorem 10).  The bound $\lambda\ge\pi\nu^{s-1}$ for maximisers is the
price--programme theorem.  For the tail statement, normalise $\sum_jA_j=1$ and sort; the amplitudes beyond the $M$-th are at
most $1/M$ each, so their mass $t$ satisfies $tM^{1-r}\le\sum_{j>M}A_j^r\le K_r^{1-r}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[$n=2$, every prime $p\ge5$]\label{cor:n2}
For $n=2$ and $2\le b\le3$, no single-frequency network (inputs in $\mathrm{span}\{\mathbf1,\cos,\sin\}$ of one frequency,
any width) beats the interference-free single-feature value at any prime $p\ge5$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
For $n=2$ the robust constant of the companion note's Theorem 14 is $k^*(2,s)=\sup\,(\Re A-3^{1-s}(\sum|\beta_i|^{1/s})^s)/(R/\sqrt2)$
with $R^2=|B|^2+|C|^2+|D|^2$, where $B,C,D$ are the three sign-flipped sums.  The four-moment inequality of the earlier notes
(Part I, Theorem A: $|A|-R/(2\sqrt3)\le3^{1-s}(\sum|\beta_i|^{1/s})^s$ for $1\le s\le3/2$, valid for arbitrary unit phases) gives
$k^*(2,s)\le\sqrt2/(2\sqrt3)=1/\sqrt6<0.41<k_5=0.946\le k_p$.  The companion note's Theorem 14 then applies.
\end{proof}
\begin{lemma}[slack identity]\label{lem:slack}
For every logit table (any network, any $p,n$, any inputs), with margin $h$ and slack
$\sigma(x,\delta):=f(x)[y(x)]-f(x)[y(x)-\delta]-h\ge0$,
\[\Phi_j(x)=\tfrac2p\Big(h-\sum_{\delta\ne0}\sigma(x,\delta)\rho^{j\delta}\Big)\qquad(j\in F).\]
Consequently every sign-flipped coefficient satisfies $|\beta_{j,T}|\le\frac2pS$ with
$S:=\mathbb E_x\sum_\delta\sigma=p\sum_{j\in F}\Re\beta_j-(p-1)h$.  The same slack $S$ bounds the interference of
\emph{every} frequency.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
$\hat f_x(j)=\sum_\delta f(x)[y-\delta]\rho^{-j(y-\delta)}=-\rho^{-jy}\sum_{\delta\ne0}(h+\sigma(x,\delta))\rho^{j\delta}$ because
$\sum_{\delta}\rho^{j\delta}=0$, and $-\sum_{\delta\ne0}\rho^{j\delta}=1$.  Then $\beta_{j,T}=\overline{\mathbb E_x\Phi_j\chi}$ for a unimodular
character $\chi$, and $|\mathbb E[\sigma\chi]|\le\mathbb E\sigma$.  For $S$, use $\mathbb E_x\Phi_j=\overline{\beta_j}$ and that
$\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z_p}\hat{\bar\sigma}(j)=p\,\bar\sigma(0)=0$.
\end{proof}
\begin{proposition}[$n=2$, any inputs]\label{prop:n2gen}
For $n=2$, $2\le b\le3$, every prime $p$ and every network, $h\le\frac p{p-1}\big(\sum_j\Re\beta_j-\max_jR_j/(2\sqrt3)\big)$,
where $R_j^2=\sum_{T\ne\emptyset}|\beta_{j,T}|^2$.  For single-feature networks (any inputs) this gives
$\gamma\le\frac{p}{p-1}\nu^{1-s}B_p$, i.e.\ $\pi_{2,b}(p)\le\nu^{1-s}\cdot\frac{p}{(p-1)\kappa_{\min}(p)}$.  This is Part I's
sharp result at $p=3$, but it is weaker than the IF value at $p\ge5$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Lemma~\ref{lem:slack} with three non-empty $T$ gives $R_j\le2\sqrt3(\sum_{j'}\Re\beta_{j'}-\frac{p-1}ph)$.  For one feature,
apply Part I's Theorem A ($|A|-R/(2\sqrt3)\le3^{1-s}(\sum|\beta_i|^{1/s})^s$; Lemma 6 of the paper supplies the unit phases for
arbitrary inputs) and the single-neuron budget.
\end{proof}
Two losses separate Proposition~\ref{prop:n2gen} from the exact answer at $p\ge5$, and both are the multi-frequency
obstacle.  (i) A single feature's gaps at its non-binding offsets carry structural slack, which uses up $S$ without any
interference.  (ii) With several frequencies, one slack budget bounds each $R_j$ but not $\sum_jR_j$, so only one
frequency's interference is charged.  Part II of the earlier notes overcomes (ii) only near $b=\nu$, by mixing the chain
matrices of all frequencies.

The general $n=2$ case (inputs with other frequencies) is also where the $L^2$ route stops.  Input energy $\varepsilon$ outside
$\pm1$ costs only $O(\varepsilon)$ of clean amplitude, but it can create non-circular (partner-mode) interference of size
$O(\sqrt\varepsilon)$.  So every $L^2$/moment bound fails in a thin regime next to the interference-free point, and a pointwise
argument, like Part I's at $p=3$, is required.

\paragraph{Numerical tests of the count with general networks.}  (i) \emph{Width.}  For slot-symmetric networks with
arbitrary input vectors (any sign patterns) and outputs on all frequencies, we searched for the smallest width with
positive margin (elimination from feasible starts, exact LP for the outputs).  It equals $\nu-\lfloor\nu/p\rfloor$ in every case
tested: $p=3$, $n=3,4,5,6,8$ (widths $3,4,4,5,6$), and $p=5$, $n=4,5,6,7$ (widths $4,5,6,7$), with one output frequency
or all of them.  So mixing frequencies does not save neurons, and Theorem~\ref{thm:width} appears to hold for all
slot-symmetric networks.
(ii) \emph{Allocation.}  At $(p,n,b)=(7,3,2)$ the programme has $K^*=2$ with unequal shares $(0.328,0,0.182)$.  Global
search (inputs by CMA-ES from interference-free blocks on every frequency subset plus perturbations, outputs by exact
conic programme with $\ell_r$ reweighting) gave: one frequency $0.801$; any two frequencies $1.00000$ with amplitude shares
equal to the programme's up to relabelling, unchanged under perturbation; all three frequencies $0.827$.  At $(5,2,2)$
($K^*=2$), random-start search reached $0.755$ (width 4, one feature), $0.821$, $0.941$, $0.970$ (widths 5--7, two features
with near-equal shares), and never exceeded the $K^*$-feature block.

\paragraph{Where a proof stops.}  Combining (a)--(c): the count prediction holds for every global optimiser, \emph{except
possibly} when $p\ge5$, $b<b_0$ and the optimiser carries interference.  None of the known counterexamples is in that set,
since they all have $p=3$.  Closing the remaining case requires a price-sharp upper bound, i.e.\ Conjecture~20$'$ or an
equivalent.  The only unconditional upper bound, Theorem 4 of the paper, is too weak: it loses the factor
$\pi\nu^{s-1}$, which swamps the differences between allocations (removing one feature changes $\Gamma_s$ by a
factor $(1-w_j)^s$ only, paper Lemma 19).  The compensation of Section~6 is the precise danger.  It uses frequencies whose
clean amplitude is tiny but non-zero and that the programme does not pay for.  An optimiser built that way would
\emph{over}count, so the strict count statement at $p\ge5$ is exactly as hard as excluding compensation at the optimum.
In every experiment at $p=5,7$, compensated networks stay $\ge4\times$ below the $K^*$-feature IF block.  Section~\ref{sec:robust} proves the count for a class that carries interference, and isolates what is left.

\section{A Lipschitz form of Lemma 7 and a transport reduction}\label{sec:robust}
\begin{lemma}[robust interference-free bound, every $n$]\label{lem:robust}
Let $0<r\le1$, $s=1/r$, $\beta_1,\dots,\beta_N\in\mathbb C$, $\xi_l\in\mathbb T^n$, $A=\sum_l\beta_l$,
$\beta_T=\sum_l\beta_l\prod_{t\in T}\xi_{lt}$, $Q=\sum_l|\beta_l|^r$, and let $\ell:=\max|\beta_T|$ over the non-empty
\emph{intervals} $T=\{t'+1,\dots,t\}\subseteq[n]$.  Then
\[\text{(i)}\ \ |A|^r\le\nu^{r-1}Q+\|L\|_{\rm op}^r,\qquad\qquad
\text{(ii)}\ \ |A|\le\nu^{1-s}Q^s+K_{\nu,s}\,\ell,\quad K_{\nu,s}:=\tfrac{\nu!-1}{\nu}\,\nu^{(s-1)(\nu-1)},\]
where $L$ is the strictly lower part of the chain matrix.  With $\ell=0$ both are the paper's Lemma 7.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $H=\sum_l\beta_lm_lm_l^*$ with the chain vectors $m_l$ of the paper's Lemma (chain).  Its diagonal is $A$, its
strictly lower entries are the interval sums $\beta_T$, so $H=H_0+L$ with $H_0=AI+U$ upper triangular and
$|L_{tt'}|\le\ell$.  Rotfel'd's inequality for $0<r\le1$ gives $\sum_k\sigma_k(H)^r\le\sum_l\|\beta_lm_lm_l^*\|_{S_r}^r=\nu^rQ$.
(i) All eigenvalues of $H_0$ equal $A$, so Weyl's majorant theorem gives $\sum_k\sigma_k(H_0)^r\ge\nu|A|^r$.  Weyl's
perturbation bound $\sigma_k(H_0)\le\sigma_k(H)+\|L\|$ and subadditivity of $t\mapsto t^r$ give
$\nu|A|^r\le\nu^rQ+\nu\|L\|^r$.
(ii) Every non-identity permutation has a factor below the diagonal, and every entry of $H$ has modulus at most
$S_1:=\sum_l|\beta_l|\le Q^s$.  So $|\det H-A^\nu|\le(\nu!-1)\,\ell\,S_1^{\nu-1}$.  By AM--GM on $\sigma_k^r$,
$|\det H|\le(\nu^{-1}\sum_k\sigma_k^r)^{\nu/r}\le a^\nu$ with $a:=\nu^{1-s}Q^s$.  Hence
$|A|^\nu\le a^\nu+(\nu!-1)\ell Q^{s(\nu-1)}$, and $(a+\varepsilon/(\nu a^{\nu-1}))^\nu\ge a^\nu+\varepsilon$ gives (ii), since
$Q^{s(\nu-1)}/a^{\nu-1}=\nu^{(s-1)(\nu-1)}$.
\end{proof}
By the paper's Lemma 6 the lemma applies to the neurons of any network at any frequency $j$, with $A=\beta_j$ and
$\ell=\ell_j:=\max_{T\text{ interval}}|\beta_{j,T}|$.

\begin{corollary}[single-frequency networks, every $n$, all large $p$]\label{cor:largep}
The constant of the companion note's Theorem 14 satisfies $k^*(n,s)\le\sqrt2\,K_{\nu,s}<\infty$ for every $n$ and $s$.
Since $k_p=\sqrt{\cot^2(\pi/p)-1}\ge p/\pi-1$ for $p\ge5$, no single-frequency network beats the interference-free
single-feature value at any prime $p\ge\pi(1+\sqrt2K_{\nu,s})$.  (For $n=2$, Corollary~\ref{cor:n2} already gives every $p\ge5$.)
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
$\Re A-\nu^{1-s}Q^s\le K_{\nu,s}\ell\le K_{\nu,s}\sqrt E=\sqrt2K_{\nu,s}\sqrt{E/2}$.  For $k_p$: $\cot x\ge1/x-x/2$ on
$(0,\pi/5]$ (the difference vanishes at $0$ and has derivative $1/x^2+1/2-\csc^2x\ge0$ there), so
$\cot^2x-1\ge x^{-2}-2\ge(x^{-1}-1)^2$ for $x\le2/3$.
\end{proof}
The constant is crude ($K_{3,3/2}=5$, $K_{4,2}=368$), so the threshold is large, but it is finite and explicit for
every $n$.

\begin{definition}
A network is \emph{frequency-pure without input DC} (class $\mathcal C$) if every neuron $i$ has a frequency $j_i\in F$ with
$u_{it}\in\mathrm{span}\{\cos(2\pi j_i\cdot/p),\sin(2\pi j_i\cdot/p)\}$ for all $t$ and
$w_i\in\mathrm{span}\{\mathbf1,\cos(2\pi j_i\cdot/p),\sin(2\pi j_i\cdot/p)\}$.  Neurons of different frequencies may
coexist in any numbers.  Every interference-free block of the paper is in $\mathcal C$.
\end{definition}
For $\theta\in\mathcal C$, expanding $u(a)\rho^{la}=p^{-1}(\hat u(-l)+\hat u(l)\rho^{2la})$ gives
$\Phi_l(x)=\sum_{T\subseteq[n]}a_{l,T}\,\rho^{2l s_T(x)}$ with $s_T(x)=\sum_{t\in T}x_t$, $a_{l,\emptyset}=\overline{\beta_l}$ and
$|a_{l,T}|=|\beta_{l,T}|$ (companion note, Lemma 11).  For $T\ne\emptyset$ define the \emph{interference profile}
\[\varphi_T(w):=\sum_{l\in F}\Re\big(a_{l,T}\rho^{lw}\big),\qquad w\in\mathbb Z_p,\]
a mean-zero real function on $\mathbb Z_p$ whose Fourier coefficients are the sign-flipped coefficients of all features at once.
Write $m_\delta:=\mathbb E_xg(x,\delta)-h\ge0$ for the mean slack and $\bar m_\delta=\frac12(m_\delta+m_{-\delta})$.

\begin{lemma}[interference profiles are slack potentials]\label{lem:potential}
Let $\theta\in\mathcal C$, $p$ any odd prime.  For every non-empty $T$, every $w\in\mathbb Z_p$ and every $\delta\ne0$,
\[\varphi_T(w)-\varphi_T(w-\delta)\ \ge\ -m_\delta .\]
Consequently $|\beta_{l,T}|\,|1-\rho^{l\delta}|\le2m_\delta$ for all $l,T,\delta$, i.e.\
$|\beta_{l,T}|\le\sqrt2\,\min(m_\delta,m_{-\delta})/\sqrt{\kappa_l(\delta)}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
With $c_l(\delta)=1-\rho^{-l\delta}$,
$\Re(a_{l,T}c_l(\delta)\rho^{2ls_T})=\Re(a_{l,T}\rho^{l\cdot2s_T})-\Re(a_{l,T}\rho^{l(2s_T-\delta)})$, so by the
companion note's Lemma 2
\[g(x,\delta)=\mathbb E_xg(\cdot,\delta)+\sum_{T\ne\emptyset}\big(\varphi_T(2s_T(x))-\varphi_T(2s_T(x)-\delta)\big).\]
Fix $T_0\ne\emptyset$ and condition on $s_{T_0}(x)=z$.  For $T\ne T_0$ the forms $s_T,s_{T_0}$ are linearly independent
over $\mathbb Z_p$, so $s_T$ is conditionally uniform and $\varphi_T$ averages to $0$.  Hence
$h\le\mathbb E[g\mid s_{T_0}=z]=h+m_\delta+\varphi_{T_0}(2z)-\varphi_{T_0}(2z-\delta)$; put $w=2z$ ($p$ odd).  For the
consequence, $w\mapsto m_\delta+\varphi_T(w)-\varphi_T(w-\delta)$ is non-negative with mean $m_\delta$, and its coefficient at
$\rho^{lw}$ is $\frac12a_{l,T}c_l(\delta)$, with $|c_l(\delta)|=\sqrt{2\kappa_l(\delta)}$.
\end{proof}
So the pointwise margin constraints, projected onto each $T$, say exactly that $\varphi_T(w')-\varphi_T(w)\le m_{w-w'}$: the
interference profile is a potential for the slack, i.e.\ $1$-Lipschitz for the path quasi-metric $D_m$ that the mean
slacks induce on the Cayley graph of $\mathbb Z_p$.  By Kantorovich--Rubinstein duality, the largest coefficient a
feature $l$ can carry at $T$ is a transport cost: $\max|\beta_{l,T}|=\frac2p\max_\phi W_{D_m}(\nu^+_{l,\phi},\nu^-_{l,\phi})$,
$\nu_{l,\phi}(w)=\cos(2\pi lw/p+\phi)$.  No circularity is used, so this holds at $p=3$ as well.

\begin{theorem}[decoupling when few features are bought cheaply]\label{thm:loc}
Let $\theta\in\mathcal C$, let $a_l:=\nu^{1-s}Q_l^{\,s}$ with $Q_l=\sum_{i}|\beta_{il}|^r$ be the interference-free price of the
amplitudes $\theta$ pays for, let $\delta^\star$ minimise $\sum_la_l\kappa_l(\delta)$, and let $L'=\{l:|\beta_l|>a_l\}$ be the
frequencies bought \emph{below} the interference-free price.  If
\[\sum_{l\in L'}\kappa_l(\delta^\star)\ \le\ \frac{2}{(2^n-1)\,k^*(n,s)^2}\qquad(\,=4\ \text{for }n=2\,),\]
then $\gamma(\theta)\le\nu^{1-s}B_p\Gamma_s(p)$, and if the inequality is strict, equality forces $\theta$ to be
interference-free.  For $n=2$ the condition holds whenever $|L'|\le2$, because $\kappa_l<2$.  Hence, for $n=2$, every odd
prime $p$ and every $b\in[2,3]$: among networks of $\mathcal C$ in which at most two frequencies are bought below the
interference-free price (any width, any interference elsewhere), the maximum is the interference-free value, and every
maximiser is interference-free and uses exactly $|\operatorname{spt}y^\star|=K^*(s,p)$ features.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Averaging $g(\cdot,\pm\delta)$ over $x$ gives, for every $\delta$, $h=\sum_l\Re\beta_l\,\kappa_l(\delta)-\bar m_\delta$.  By the
companion note's Theorem 14 (whose constant is finite by Corollary~\ref{cor:largep}),
$|\beta_l|-a_l\le k^*\sqrt{E_l/2}$ with $E_l=\sum_{T\ne\emptyset}|\beta_{l,T}|^2$.  For $n=2$ take $k^*=1/\sqrt6$ (Part I, Theorem A).
Fix $T$.  By the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:potential}, $D_T(w):=\varphi_T(w)-\varphi_T(w-\delta^\star)$ has mean zero and values in
$[-m_{\delta^\star},m_{-\delta^\star}]$ (the upper bound is the constraint at $(w-\delta^\star,-\delta^\star)$).  So
$\frac1p\sum_wD_T^2\le m_{\delta^\star}m_{-\delta^\star}\le\bar m^2$ (Bhatia--Davis), and Parseval, with coefficients
$a_{l,T}c_l(\delta^\star)$ and $|c_l|^2=2\kappa_l$, gives $\sum_l|\beta_{l,T}|^2\kappa_l(\delta^\star)\le\bar m^2$.  Summing over $T$:
$\sum_lE_l\kappa_l(\delta^\star)\le(2^n-1)\bar m^2$.  By Cauchy--Schwarz,
\[h\le\sum_la_l\kappa_l(\delta^\star)+\frac{k^*}{\sqrt2}\sum_{l\in L'}\sqrt{E_l}\,\kappa_l(\delta^\star)-\bar m
\le\sum_la_l\kappa_l(\delta^\star)+\bar m\Big(k^*\sqrt{\tfrac{2^n-1}2\textstyle\sum_{l\in L'}\kappa_l(\delta^\star)}-1\Big).\]
Under the condition the bracket is $\le0$, so $h\le\nu^{1-s}\min_\delta\sum_lQ_l^{\,s}\kappa_l(\delta)\le\nu^{1-s}(C_bP)^s\Gamma_s$
by the single-neuron budget $\sum_lQ_l\le C_bP$.  If the bracket is $<0$ and equality holds, then $\bar m=0$, and
Lemma~\ref{lem:potential} gives $\beta_{l,T}=0$ for all $l,T$.  So the maximiser is interference-free, and the paper's
Theorem 8 gives its amplitudes and its count.
\end{proof}
Theorem~\ref{thm:loc} is the first count result at $p\ge5$ outside the dense regime for a class of networks that carry
interference.  Any number of features may carry interference, and up to two of them (for $n=2$) may use it to buy
themselves below the interference-free price.  They still cannot beat the interference-free value, because the slack
their interference forces at $\delta^\star$ is at least as large as what they gain.  What it does not cover is
\emph{collective} undercutting: many features each bought a little below price, with phases arranged so that their
profiles nearly cancel.  The proof charges these through Cauchy--Schwarz over $L'$, which loses $\sqrt{|L'|}$.  A proof of
the general count must replace this step by an estimate that sees the cancellation.

\paragraph{Answer to ``prove that the unrestricted optimum matches the prediction''.}  In the interference-free form this
cannot be done: the statement is false at $p=3$, and increasingly so as $n$ grows.  In the price--programme form it holds at
$p=3$ by definition, and there the object to compute is $\pi_{n,b}(3)$.  We have shown that, in the class that contains every
counterexample, this reduces to choosing $\nu-\lfloor\nu/3\rfloor$ real slopes, with the weights given by an LP.  For
$p\ge5$ the full statement would follow from two claims:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(Q1)] $\pi_{n,b}(p)=\nu^{1-s}$, i.e.\ a single feature cannot be bought below the IF price.  Theorem~\ref{thm:width}
limits the width saving of the $p=3$ mechanism to $\lfloor\nu/p\rfloor$ neurons, and to none for $p>\nu$.  The circularity
theorem of the companion note handles single-frequency inputs when $k_p\ge k^*$.  A proof needs a cost-weighted (robust)
form of the width theorem for arbitrary sign patterns.
\item[(Q2)] Decoupling.  It has to control compensation by interference-only fields, which demonstrably raises a feature's
margin.
\end{enumerate}
Both hold in all our experiments at $p=5,7$, by margins of $4\times$ or more that grow with $n$.
