Conjecture 20 is false

At p = 3 and weight decay (b = 2), a product network that carries interference beats the interference-free value of Theorem 8. Two explicit networks, at n = 11 and n = 13, are certified in exact rational arithmetic.

Answer. The maximum L2,b-margin over all product networks is not always the interference-free value ν1−sBpΓs(p). The smallest counterexample found has p = 3, n = 11, b = 2 and only 8 neurons (the interference-free optimum needs ν = 12). The violation grows with n and also covers b slightly above 2.

Theorem 8 itself is unaffected. What fails is the claim that no interfering network can do better.

How it gets around Lemma 7

Each input factor has the form ui(a) ∝ κi + 2 cos(2πa/3 + φi): a single cosine plus a constant (DC) offset. The interference-free block of Proposition 9 is the case κ = 0 with ν neurons whose phases cover 180°. The counterexample departs from it in two ways.

  • Fewer neurons. 8 neurons whose phases are spaced about 14° apart on a 98° arc centred at 180°. With fewer than ν neurons, Lemma 7 says the sign-flipped copies cannot cancel.
  • DC offsets. κi runs from 0.50 in the centre of the arc to 0.76 at its ends. A constant component creates the Fourier modes with kt = 0, which Theorem 8 leaves unconstrained. These modes push the leftover interference back inside the gap cone {g(x, 1), g(x, 2) ≥ h} at every input. The margin binds on 32 of the 78 count classes.

The accounting is simple. By Parseval a DC slot costs |û(0)|² against 2|û(1)|², so a neuron with offset κ costs (1 + κ²/2) times a centred neuron of the same amplitude. Here 8 × 1.185 ≈ 9.5 centred-neuron equivalents replace the block's 12. At b = 2 the normalized margin scales like (budget per neuron)ν/2, so this saving beats the loss in efficiency once ν is large enough. In the paper's notation the neuron price is ν1−r with r = 2/ν, and the counterexample avoids paying it in full.

What still stands

  • Proved and unaffected. Theorem 8 and Proposition 9 (the interference-free optimum), the dense endpoint b = ν (Corollary 14), Theorems 10–11 and Corollary 12 (orders in p at fixed ν, b). Also the earlier full proof at p = 3, n = 2 for all b ∈ [2, 3], and the interval b ∈ [b0, ν] from the matrix proof.
  • Numerically intact at small n. At b = 2, searches over all real product networks found nothing above the interference-free value at p = 3, n = 3–6; p = 5, n = 2–3; and p = 7, n = 2. Those searches used widths up to 14, random and adversarial starts, and SLSQP polishing. Centred networks (no DC components) with ν − 1 neurons reach only 0.20 at n = 3 and 0.16 at n = 4. The boxed case h ≤ P²/768 at n = 3 is therefore still plausible, but unproved.
  • A corrected statement has to depend on n and b. For each p, n there should be a threshold b*(p, n) ∈ [2, b0] above which interference cannot help. At p = 3 we have b*(3, 11) ≈ 2.05 and b*(3, 12) ≈ 2.16. Whether b*(3, n) = 2 for every n ≤ 10 is open.

Why the earlier proofs could not have extended

Both proof routes in the progress notes bound the margin using two ingredients only: the clean and sign-flipped Fourier modes of the score table, and the single-neuron Fourier budget. Every mode with a constant slot is left free. That relaxation is already false at n = 3, where the conjecture may still hold.
