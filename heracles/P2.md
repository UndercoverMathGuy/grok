## 1. Exact statements

> **THEOREM P2-1 (proved; every `n >= 1`, every `r in (0,1]`, every `eta >= 0`; `N = n+1`).**
> For a population of `N = n+1` atoms with `C_empty = 1` and junk energy at most `eta^2`,
> write `delta = sqrt(2^n - 1) eta`. Then the moduli `p_l = |c_l|` satisfy, simultaneously,
>
>     (C1)  sum_l p_l >= 1 ;
>     (C2)  prod_l p_l >= ((1 - delta)_+ / (n+1))^{n+1} ;
>     (C3)  p_l >= (1 - delta)_+ / 2^n   for every l ,
>
> and consequently
>
>     Cl2_r(eta)|_{N = n+1}  >=  L_r(eta) := min { sum_l x_l^r : x satisfies (C1),(C2),(C3) } .
>
> `L_r` is an explicit `(n+1)`-variable concave minimisation, computed exactly in
> `p2_bound.py`. `L_r(0) = (n+1)^{1-r}` exactly, so the minorant is **tight at zero junk**.
>
> *Plain reading: the clean amplitude being exactly `1` forces the atom moduli to add up to
> at least `1`; the Key Lemma's Vandermonde/Hadamard chain, run with junk, forces their
> product to stay near `(n+1)^{-(n+1)}`; and no single atom can be too small. The cheapest
> set of moduli obeying all three is the minorant.*

> **THEOREM P2-2 (proved; quantitative Hadamard; every `n`).**
> For `n+1` unit-modulus numbers `v_1..v_{n+1}` with power sums `P_k = sum_l v_l^k`,
>
>     V^2 := prod_{l<m} |v_l - v_m|^2  <=  (n+1)^{n+1} exp( - (1/(n+1)^3) sum_{k=1}^{n} (n+1-k) |P_k|^2 ) .
>
> *Plain reading: Hadamard's inequality is an equality only at rotated roots of unity, where
> every power sum `P_1..P_n` vanishes; this says how much is lost per unit of surviving power
> sum, and it is the only "stability of Hadamard" statement the programme has.*

> **THE REDUCTION (proved, §5): P2 follows from QPL.** Define
>
>     Pi(eta) := min { (n+1)^{n+1} prod_{l=1}^{n+1} |c_l| : C_empty = 1, junk energy <= eta^2 } .
>
> **(QPL)**   `Pi(eta) >= exp(-c_n eta^2)` with `c_n = (n+1)^2/(4n)`, for `eta <= eta_prod(n)`.
>
> Then Theorem P2-1 with `(C2)` replaced by `prod_l p_l >= (n+1)^{-(n+1)} exp(-c_n eta^2)`
> gives a **quadratic**, zero-slope-at-zero minorant of `Cl2_r`, and the trade closes below
> the explicit thresholds of §6–§7.
>
> Status of QPL: the constant `c_n = (n+1)^2/(4n)` is *derived* (it is the exact value of the
> second-order programme, §4.2) and *measured* (§4.1). The statement itself is a conjecture
> supported by 35 junk levels x 2 values of `n`; §4.3–§4.4 prove one of its two halves and
> pin the missing half to a single cross-slot inequality.

## 3. Theorem P2-1: the proven minorant, and exactly how far short it falls

### 3.1 The three constraints

Throughout `N = n+1`, `C_empty = sum_l c_l = 1`, junk energy `<= eta^2`, and
`delta := sqrt(2^n - 1) * eta`.

**(C1) `sum_l p_l >= 1`.** The triangle inequality applied to `sum_l c_l = 1`. *Plain
reading: the atoms have to add up to the clean amplitude, so their moduli cannot add up to
less.* This is the constraint nobody had used, and it is the one that does not degrade with
junk at all.

**(C0) The junk polynomial is small everywhere.** Write `h = 1 + g`, `g(u) = sum_{T != empty}
C_T u^T`. For any `u` on the torus, Cauchy–Schwarz over the `2^n - 1` junk patterns gives
`|g(u)| <= sqrt(2^n - 1) * sqrt(sum_{T != empty}|C_T|^2) <= delta`. Hence
`R := min_{T^n} Re h >= 1 - delta`, and also `|h(u)| >= 1 - delta` everywhere.

**(C2) `prod_l p_l >= ((1-delta)_+/(n+1))^{n+1}`.** This is Theorem B's chain
(`GLOBAL_OPT` §4 (4.2)), which needs nothing but `R`: bijection kill points, geometric mean
over the `n!` bijections, product over the `n+1` choices of survivor, Hadamard on each slot's
Vandermonde. With `R >= 1 - delta` from (C0) it reads as stated.

**(C3) `p_l >= (1-delta)_+/2^n` for every `l`.** The same bijection identity (4.1) without the
averaging: `|h(u^{l0,sigma})| = p_{l0} prod_j d^{(j)}_{sigma(j),l0}` with every chord
`d <= 2`, so `p_{l0} >= |h(u^{l0,sigma})|/2^n >= (1-delta)/2^n`.

All three hold simultaneously, so `Cl2_r|_{N=n+1}(eta) >= L_r(eta)`, the value of the
`(n+1)`-variable programme. `L_r(0) = (n+1)^{1-r}`: at `delta = 0`, (C2) alone forces
`sum x^r >= (n+1)(prod x)^{r/(n+1)} >= (n+1)^{1-r}` by AM–GM, with equality at
`x = (1/(n+1),...)`, which also satisfies (C1) and (C3). ∎

### 3.2 What it is worth

Write `A(eta) = Cl2_r(eta)^{1/r}`, `kappa0 = A(0) = (n+1)^{(1-r)/r}` and

    rho(eta) := [1 - A(eta)/kappa0] / eta .

P3A's criterion (its §2.3) is `sup_eta rho(eta) <= rho_need := (1 - 1/kappa0)/sqrt(2^n-1)`;
agent 4's `K`-dependent (JT) is `rho(eta) <= 1/(2 K sqrt(2^n - 1))` up to the junk level in
question.

Small-`eta` expansion of `L_r` (done by hand and confirmed by `p2_bound.py`): (C1) and (C2)
are the binding pair, (C3) is slack at the block, and the AM–GM deficit argument gives

    L_r(eta) = (n+1)^{1-r} [ 1 - r(1-r) delta + O(delta^2) ] ,
    hence  A(eta) >= kappa0 [ 1 - (1-r) delta ],   rho(0+) = (1-r) sqrt(2^n - 1) .

**This is a real advance and an honest failure.** Advance: the inherited proved relief
`Cl2_r(eta) >= (n+1)^{1-r}(1 - (2^n-1)eta)^r` gives `rho <= 2^n - 1 = 3` at `nu = 3, q = 2`,

is needed, which is the whole point of the problem and the content of §4.

### 3.3 Where the linear loss sits, exactly

Every one of the four inherited proofs, and Theorem P2-1 too, prices junk through the single
step `R >= 1 - delta`. That step is *sharp as a statement about `R`*: a multi-affine `g` with
all `2^n - 1` junk coefficients of equal modulus `eta/sqrt(2^n-1)`, aligned at one torus
point, really does have `min Re h = 1 - delta`. So the loss is not in (C0); it is in feeding
`R` into (C2). At the perturbed block the truth is

    prod_l p_l = (n+1)^{-(n+1)} (1 - O(eta^2)),    while (C2) only gives (1 - O(eta)) .

The first-order term in `prod_l p_l` vanishes because `sum_l c_l = 1` forces `sum_l x_l = 0`
for the modulus perturbations `p_l = (1 + x_l)/(n+1)`. **The product, not the cost, is where
the missing square is**, and that is what §4 isolates.

## 4. The Quadratic Product Lemma: the whole of P2 in one `r`-free statement

### 4.1 The object

    Pi(eta) := min { (n+1)^{n+1} prod_{l=1}^{n+1} |c_l| }
               over  C_empty = 1  and junk energy <= eta^2 .

`Pi(0) = 1` is the Key Lemma's product step. `Pi` does **not** depend on `r`, nor on `p`, nor
on `K`: it is a minimisation of a *product* over `n+1` complex atoms and `n(n+1)` phases. It
is a strictly simpler object than `Cl2_r` (which also minimises over `N`), and by Theorem
P2-1's algebra it *determines* a minorant of `Cl2_r` at `N = n+1`:

> **THEOREM P2-3 (proved; the reduction).** For every `n`, `r in (0,1]` and `eta >= 0`,
>
>     Cl2_r|_{N = n+1}(eta)  >=  max{ 1, Prog_r(eta) } ,
>     Prog_r(eta) := min { sum_l x_l^r : sum_l x_l >= 1,
>                          prod_l x_l >= Pi(eta)/(n+1)^{n+1},
>                          x_l >= (1 - sqrt(2^n-1) eta)_+ / 2^n } .
>
> *Plain reading: once you know how far the atoms' product can fall, the rest of P2 is a
> three-line `(n+1)`-variable programme.* Theorem P2-1 is the special case
> `Pi(eta) >= (1 - sqrt(2^n-1)eta)_+^{n+1}` (the only bound previously available).

**Measured `Pi`** (`p2_qpl.py`, SLSQP with analytic Jacobians, 40 starts per level,
`N = n+1`). At `n = 2` (`etastar = sqrt 3 = 1.7321`):

| `-log Pi` | 1.01e-5 | 1.13e-4 | 1.03e-3 | 4.20e-3 | 1.20e-2 | 3.07e-2 | 5.97e-2 | 0.1013 | 0.1591 | 0.2387 | 0.3507 | 0.5193 | 0.8309 | **0 (`Pi = 0`)** |

Two readings. (i) **The deficit is exactly quadratic at the origin**, with limiting constant
`1.125 = 9/8` — matching §4.2 below to three digits at `eta = 3e-3`. (ii) `Pi` reaches `0`

survives. That is an intrinsic limit of the product, recorded here so nobody expects the
`N = n+1` product argument to reach the endpoint.

### 4.2 The sharp constant, in closed form

> **PROPOSITION P2-4 (proved: exact at `n = 2` by hand, and as an eigenvalue for `n <= 6`).**
> The second-order programme at the block has value
>
>     c_n  =  (n+1)^2 / (4 n) ,
>
> i.e. `-log Pi(eta) = c_n eta^2 + O(eta^3)`. Values: `1` (`n=1`), `9/8` (`n=2`), `4/3`
> (`n=3`), `25/16` (`n=4`), `9/5` (`n=5`), `49/24` (`n=6`).

**Derivation.** Put `c_l = (1 + eps_l)/(n+1)` with `eps_l = x_l + i y_l`, and
`v_lj = w^{-l} e^{i delta_lj}` with `w = exp(2 pi i/(n+1))`. Then, to second order,

    -log[ (n+1)^{n+1} prod_l |c_l| ]  =  (1/2)( sum_l x_l^2 - sum_l y_l^2 ) ,
    C_empty = 1   <=>   sum_l eps_l = 0 ,
    C_T          =  (1/(n+1)) sum_l w^{-l|T|} ( eps_l + i sum_{j in T} delta_lj ) ,  T != empty

(the `sum_l w^{-l|T|} = 0` for `1 <= |T| <= n` is what removes the zeroth order). So

    c_n = max { (1/2)(sum x^2 - sum y^2) : sum_l eps_l = 0, sum_{T != empty}|C_T|^2 <= 1 } ,

a quadratic-over-quadratic programme; equivalently the least `lam >= 0` with
`lam Q - P` positive semidefinite, `Q` the junk form and `P` the objective form. `Q` is
singular — its kernel is the junk-free perturbation space, on which `P <= 0` by the Key
Lemma — so one may **not** restrict to `range(Q)`; the kernel/range cross terms are exactly
what makes `c_n` bigger than the naive `range(Q)` eigenvalue (`0.9107` instead of `1.125` at
`n = 2`; recorded because it cost me a wrong first number).

*Exact solution at `n = 2`.* Write `X_t = sum_l w^{-lt} x_l`, `Y_t` likewise, and
`a_j = sum_l w^{-l} delta_lj`. Reality of `x, y, delta` gives `X_2 = conj(X_1)` etc.
Eliminating the phases: with `P := X_1`, `R := Y_1`, `A := P + iR`, `B := P - iR` and
`w := i(a_1 + a_2)`, the junk energy is

    9 * (junk)  =  |A + s|^2 + |A + u|^2 + |B - w|^2 ,   s + u = w ,

minimised at `s = u = w/2` and then at `w = (2/3)(B - A) = -(4/3) i R`, giving
`9*(junk) = 3|P + iR/3|^2`. The objective is `(|P|^2 - |R|^2)/3`. Setting `P = p > 0`,
`R = i rho`, the programme is `max (p^2 - rho^2)/3` subject to `(p - rho/3)^2 <= 3`, whose
optimum is `rho = 3 sqrt3/8`, `p = sqrt3 + sqrt3/8`, value `(27/8)/3 = 9/8`. ∎

*General `n`*: `p2_secord.py` builds `P` and `Q` and bisects on the PSD condition. Result:
`c_n / [(n+1)^2/(4n)] = 1.000000000` in **6/6 cells** (`n = 1..6`).

**Plain reading of the mechanism.** The atoms' *moduli* being unequal is what makes the
product fall, and the embedding phases `delta_lj` are free currency with which the junk that
unequal moduli create can be partly (never wholly) cancelled. `c_n` is exactly the exchange
rate. The optimal attack does not use real modulus perturbations alone — it uses an imaginary
part `R` (which costs the objective) precisely because it buys a better cancellation; that is
why the answer is `9/8` and not `1`.

### 4.3 Theorem P2-2: Hadamard's inequality is quantitatively slack

The only proof route to `Pi` the programme has is the Key Lemma's chain. Written exactly
(no inequality yet spent), with `H` the average of `log|h|` over all `(n+1)!` kill points and
`V_j` slot `j`'s Vandermonde product,

    log[ (n+1)^{n+1} prod_l |c_l| ]  =  (n+1) H  +  Delta ,
    Delta := (n+1) log(n+1) - (1/n) sum_j log V_j^2  >=  0   (Hadamard).

So QPL is exactly the statement `(n+1) H + Delta >= -c_n eta^2`: **the Hadamard defect
`Delta` must pay for whatever junk is visible at the kill points.** Theorem P2-2 supplies the
first half — a defect with an explicit rate:

    V_j^2  <=  (n+1)^{n+1} exp( -(n+1)^{-3} sum_{k=1}^{n} (n+1-k) |P_k^{(j)}|^2 ) ,
    P_k^{(j)} = sum_l (v_lj)^k ,

hence `Delta >= (1/(n (n+1)^3)) sum_j sum_k (n+1-k)|P_k^{(j)}|^2`.

*Proof.* Let `W_{a,l} = (v_lj)^a`, `a = 0..n`; then `det(W W*) = V_j^2` and
`(W W*)_{ab} = P_{a-b}^{(j)}`. Put `A = W W*/(n+1)`: PSD, unit diagonal, trace `n+1`, so
every eigenvalue lies in `(0, n+1]`. For `lam in (0, Lam]`,
`log lam <= (lam - 1) - (lam - 1)^2/(2 Lam)` (both sides and their first derivatives agree at
`lam = 1` and at `lam = Lam`, and the difference is monotone on each side of `1`). Summing
over eigenvalues, with `sum(lam - 1) = 0`,
`log det A <= -||A - I||_F^2/(2(n+1))`, and
`||A - I||_F^2 = (n+1)^{-2} sum_{a != b} |P_{a-b}|^2 = (n+1)^{-2} * 2 sum_{k=1}^{n}(n+1-k)|P_k|^2`. ∎

*Check* (`p2_hadamard.py`, 4000 random configurations per `n`): `max V_j^2/bound` is
`0.999999999` (`n=1`), `0.999175698` (`n=2`), `0.997444494` (`n=3`), `0.914505609` (`n=4`) —
never above `1`, and essentially tight at `n = 1, 2`.

### 4.4 Where QPL's proof breaks, precisely

The other half needs `H`, the kill-point average of `log|h| = log|1 + g|`, bounded below.
Its first-order term is `sum_{T != empty} Re( C_T m_T )` with `m_T` the average of `u^T` over
the `(n+1)!` kill points, so `H >= -eta ||m||_2 + O(eta^2)` by Cauchy–Schwarz. Both moments
are computable in closed form (`p2_hadamard.py`, verified to `3.6e-16`):

    m_{ {j} }   =  -conj(S_j)/(n+1) ,             S_j := sum_l v_lj  (= P_1^{(j)})    [every n]
    m_{ {1,2} } =  ( conj(S_1) conj(S_2) - Z )/6 , Z := sum_l conj(v_l1 v_l2)          [n = 2]

The `|T| = 1` moments are *exactly* the quantities Theorem P2-2 prices: if only those were
present, combining `H >= -eta sigma` (where `sigma^2 = sum_j |S_j|^2/(n+1)^2`) with
`Delta >= sigma^2/n` would prove QPL outright, with the (worse but explicit) constant
`c_n^{proved} = n(n+1)^2/4` — the maximum over `sigma` of `[(n+1) eta sigma - sigma^2/n]/eta^2`.

**It is the cross-slot moment `Z` that breaks it.** Take `v_l = (w^{-l}, w^{+l})`: every
per-slot power sum vanishes, so `Delta = 0` and Theorem P2-2 gives nothing, yet `Z = 3` and
`|m_{1,2}| = 1/2` (verified). So no bound of the form "kill-point loss `<=` Hadamard defect"
can hold.

That configuration is nevertheless harmless, and the reason says what the missing lemma is:
for it, `C_{ {1,2} } = sum_l c_l w^{-2l} w^{+2l}`... more simply `prod_j v_lj = 1` for every
`l`, so `C_{ {1,2} } = sum_l c_l = C_empty = 1` **identically**, i.e. `eta >= 1` whatever the
atoms are. The cross moment is not controlled by the Hadamard defect — it is controlled by
*the junk itself*. So:

> **The one missing step of P2.** A bound of the shape
> `sum_{T, |T| >= 2} |m_T|^2  <=  const * (Delta + eta^2)`,
> i.e. the higher kill-point moments are paid for either by the Hadamard defect or by the
> junk already present. With it, QPL follows with an explicit constant by the `sigma`
> computation above, and by Theorem P2-3 the whole of P2 follows for `N = n+1`.

## 5. What QPL buys: a closed-form quadratic minorant

Theorem P2-3's programme is solved numerically in §6–§7, but the *clean* consequence needs
only AM–GM and is worth stating on its own, because it is the sentence the paper would quote.

> **COROLLARY P2-5 (proved from QPL; no numerics).** Suppose
> `-log Pi(eta) <= C eta^2` on `[0, eta_0]`. Then for every `r in (0,1]` and `eta <= eta_0`,
>
>     Cl2_r|_{N=n+1}(eta)  >=  (n+1)^{1-r} exp( - r C eta^2/(n+1) ) ,
>
> equivalently `A(eta) >= kappa0 exp(-C eta^2/(n+1))`, and therefore
>
>     rho(eta) := [1 - A(eta)/kappa0]/eta   <=   C eta/(n+1) .
>
> *Proof.* AM–GM on the `r`-th powers: `sum_l x_l^r >= (n+1)(prod_l x_l^r)^{1/(n+1)}
> = (n+1)(prod_l x_l)^{r/(n+1)} >= (n+1) * [(n+1)^{-(n+1)} e^{-C eta^2}]^{r/(n+1)}`. The
> last display uses `1 - e^{-t} <= t`. ∎
>
> **The `rho` bound is linear in `eta` with an `r`-free slope `C/(n+1)`.** That is the whole
> point: the two criteria ask `rho` to stay below a *constant*, so a slope gives a
> *threshold*, whereas Theorem P2-1's constant `rho` gives none.

With the sharp constant `C = c_n = (n+1)^2/(4n)` the slope is `(n+1)/(4n)`:
`0.375` at `n = 2`, `0.3333` at `n = 3`. The resulting closed-form thresholds are

    chord (P3A, K-free):   eta  <=  4 n rho_need / (n+1) ,
    (JT)  (agent 4, K):    eta  <=  4 n / [ 2 K (n+1) sqrt(2^n - 1) ]  =  2n / [K(n+1)sqrt(2^n-1)] .

| cell | `rho_need` | slope `c_n/(n+1)` | chord threshold | `(JT)` threshold |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `nu=3, q=2` | 0.2440169 | 0.375000 | `eta <= 0.6507` | `eta <= 0.7698/K` |
| `nu=3, q=2.5` | 0.1138872 | 0.375000 | `eta <= 0.3037` | `eta <= 0.7698/K` |
| `nu=4, q=2` | 0.2834734 | 0.333333 | `eta <= 0.8504` | `eta <= 0.5670/K` |
| `nu=4, q=3` | 0.1398618 | 0.333333 | `eta <= 0.4196` | `eta <= 0.5670/K` |

**Honest caveat on the range.** `C = c_n` is the limiting constant at `eta = 0` only; the
measured `-log Pi(eta)/eta^2` already reaches `1.20` at `eta = 0.10` and `1.83` at
`eta = 0.44` (`n = 2`), so the table above is the *tangent* statement. Using the measured
`C(eta_0) = sup_{eta <= eta_0} (-log Pi)/eta^2` and solving the resulting fixed point
`eta = (n+1) rho_need / C(eta)` gives the self-consistent chord threshold `eta <= 0.41` at
`nu = 3, q = 2`. §7 reports the sharper numbers from the full programme, which also uses
`sum_l x_l >= 1` and so beats Corollary P2-5.

## 6. The trade, redone

### 6.1 The two criteria, stated so they can be checked on any minorant

Both inherited criteria are conditions on the single function
`rho(eta) = [1 - A(eta)/kappa0]/eta`, `A = Cl2_r^{1/r}`, `kappa0 = (n+1)^{(1-r)/r}`:

* **(JT)** (agent 4, `GLOBAL_OPT4` §6.1). On a `K`-frequency support, (T) holds on the pure
  class for every junk profile with `eta_k <= eta_cap` as soon as
  `rho(eta) <= 1/(2 K sqrt(2^n - 1))` for all `eta <= eta_cap`.
  *Plain reading: the left side of the original (JT) is what one junky frequency loses on the
  margin, diluted by having to share a single Fourier probe with `K` frequencies; the right
  side is what the whole support gains on cost.*
* **(CH)** (P3A §2.3, `K`-free). (T) holds at **every** junk level and **every** `K` as soon
  as `rho(eta) <= rho_need := (1 - 1/kappa0)/sqrt(2^n - 1)` on all of `(0, etastar]`.
  Geometrically: `A` must lie above the chord joining `(0, kappa0)` to `(etastar, 1)`.
  `rho_need = 0.2440169` (`nu3 q2`), `0.1138872` (`nu3 q2.5`), `0.2834734` (`nu4 q2`),
  `0.1398618` (`nu4 q3`).

A **constant** `rho` (which is what any minorant linear in `eta` and tight at `0` produces)
either satisfies a criterion everywhere or nowhere. A **linear** `rho` (which is what a
quadratic minorant produces) gives a genuine threshold. That single observation is the
difference between §3 and §5.

## 8. Approaches tried here that FAILED, and exactly where

**F2-1. A chord/concavity argument on `Cl2_r` directly. Dead as a soft argument.** P3A's
criterion is exactly "`A(eta) = Cl2_r(eta)^{1/r}` lies above the chord joining `(0, kappa0)`
and `(etastar, 1)`", which would follow from concavity of `A`. `A` is **not** concave in any
way one can get for free: the natural mixing construction — take a population `P` with junk
`eta` and a junk-free block `B`, scale them by `1-u` and `u` and take the union — gives
`Cl2_r((1-u)eta) <= (1-u)^r Cl2_r(eta) + u^r (n+1)^{1-r}`, an **upper** bound on `Cl2_r` at a
*smaller* junk level. Rearranged it reads
`Cl2_r(eta) >= [Cl2_r((1-u)eta) - u^r (n+1)^{1-r}]/(1-u)^r`, and since `u^r >> u` for `r < 1`
the correction beats the gain for every small `u`: the inequality is vacuous. The same
happens for the *partial gauge symmetrisation* mixture (weights `1-u+u/(n+1)`, `u/(n+1)`,
... on the `n+1` rotations `psi_i` that kill every junk pattern at once): the junk scales by
`1-u`, the cost by `(1-2u/3)^r + 2(u/3)^r`, and at `u = 1` one recovers exactly the trivial
`Cl2_r >= 1`, at `u -> 0` nothing. **Recorded because both look like they should work.**

**F2-2. Robustifying `ADVERSARY` §6 (P1 for every `N`) to get P2 for every `N`. Dead at
`tau = 0`, i.e. it never reaches the hard case.** That argument's three ingredients
robustify cleanly — Theorem B on the top `n+1` atoms with `R_S >= 1 - 2^n tau - sqrt(2^n-1)eta`,
the peeling ladder with `sum_{l>h} x_l >= D_h(1 - kappa_h eta)` by Cauchy–Schwarz (see F2-3),
and the tail price `Tail(tau, beta)` which does not see junk at all. But the minimum of the
resulting `G_eta(beta, tau)` is attained at `tau = 0`, where the whole thing collapses to
plain Theorem B and degrades *linearly*. The `2.2%` of margin that `ADVERSARY` reports at
`q = 2` is margin in the `tau >= eps*/2` region only, and buys nothing at `tau = 0`. **So
P2 at `N > n+1` is not harder than P2 at `N = n+1`: it is the same problem.** That is the
one genuinely good piece of news about the `N` axis.

**F2-3. The peeling ladder with an `l_2` (rather than `l_1`) junk robustification. Correct,
new, and useless at small `eta`.** For any normalised multi-affine `Lambda`,
`sum_T lambda_T C_T = sum_l c_l Lambda(v_l)`, so
`|Lambda|_inf sum_{l not in S} |c_l| >= 1 - ||lambda_{!= empty}||_2 * eta` by Cauchy–Schwarz
— strictly better than the inherited `1 - (2^n-1)*max_T|C_T|`. I computed the optimal
`l_2`-norms at the block: killing one atom at `n = 2` needs
`Lambda = 1 - (1/4)z_1 - (1/4)z_2 - (1/2)z_1z_2` (up to the atom's rotation), giving
`|Lambda(v_l)| = 3/2` at the survivors and `||lambda||_2 = sqrt(3/8) = 0.6124`; killing two
needs `||lambda||_2 = sqrt(3/2) = 1.2247` with `|Lambda| = 3`. **But at the block these
constraints are slack** (`x_2 + x_3 = 2/3 > D_1 = 3sqrt3/8 = 0.6495`,
`x_3 = 1/3 > D_2 = 1/4`), so they do not bind at `eta = 0` and cannot improve the slope
there. They only matter at large `eta`, where the product constraint has already died. (The
slackness is exact arithmetic at the block, not a measurement; I did not re-run the
programme with them added, because a constraint slack at the minimiser cannot change its
value.)

**F2-4. `min_{T^n} Re h >= 1 - c eta` with `c < sqrt(2^n - 1)`. REFUTED.** One might hope the
Cauchy–Schwarz step `|g(u)| <= sqrt(2^n-1) eta` is loose. It is not: a multi-affine `g` whose
`2^n - 1` junk coefficients all have modulus `eta/sqrt(2^n-1)` and align at one torus point
achieves it exactly. **The linear loss is genuinely in `R`, not in the bound on `R`** — which
is why the fix has to bypass `R` and go to the product (§4).

**F2-5. Averaging `log|1 + g|` over the kill points and hoping for Jensen. Fails, but
instructively.** If the `(n+1)!` kill points were Haar distributed on `T^n`, Lemma M
(`GLOBAL_OPT4` §4) would give `E log|h| >= log|C_empty| = 0` and QPL would follow with `c = 0`
— no junk penalty at all. They are not Haar: their moments `m_T` are given in closed form in
§4.4, and they vanish *at the block* but not in general. The correct statement of the gap is
therefore "the kill-point measure is Haar to first order at the block", which is exactly the
rigidity that §4.4's missing lemma would supply.

**Previously recorded dead routes** (Morwani-style dual certificate, the domination lemma,
the capped-mass conjecture `(C')`, gauge symmetrisation, grid rotation, Gram/Carathéodory–
Fejér, Mahler for `N > n+1`, one averaged test measure / Theorem O, the variance junk lemma):
none re-attempted, none changed.
