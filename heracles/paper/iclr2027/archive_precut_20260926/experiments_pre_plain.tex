We train the networks of Section~\ref{sec:prelim} with gradient descent and ask whether the number of features tracks the programme \eqref{eq:programme}. Theorem~\ref{thm:cyclic} is a statement about interference-free maximizers. Yet trained networks are neither interference-free nor exact maximizers, hence, we predict trends in the number of frequencies observed and not exact counts. Training details, the convergence criterion and the count definition are in Appendix~\ref{app:exp}.

\subsection{Where interference enters trained networks}

Trained networks carry some interference: most of their logit energy lies in the clean modes $(j,\dots,j;-j)$ (median $0.97$), and less of it as $s$ grows. They are also not maximizers of the programme. Their learned allocations reach about $0.87$ of the optimum $\Gamma_s(p)$, well above a random allocation or the network at initialization, but the individual budget shares do not match $y^*$. No trained network exceeded the universal bound of Theorem~\ref{thm:universal}. The numbers behind this section are in Appendix~\ref{app:exp}.

\subsection{The exponent \texorpdfstring{$s=\nu/b$}{s = nu/b}}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/fig_exponent.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{The count follows the exponent.} (a) Product networks at $\nu=4$, $p=37$, sweeping $b$ from $4$ to $2$ ($s$ from $1$ to $2$). Black: $\Kstar(s)$, exact, with its twelve plateaus (the two at $\Kstar=6$ differ in their amplitude ratios). Each marker is one run ($19$ converged, $13$ not). (b) Every other sweep in $b$ at fixed $(\nu,p)$ with at least two converged cells ($\nu=4$, $p=59$ has one and is omitted). Black: $\Kstar$; colour: median count over converged seeds; dots: individual seeds. The $s$ values are drawn equally spaced.}
\label{fig:exponent}
\end{figure}

Sweeping the norm exponent at fixed degree and prime (Figure~\ref{fig:exponent}a), the trained count falls with $\Kstar$, from every frequency at $b=\nu$ to three at $b=2$, although it does not resolve the finer plateaus of $\Kstar$. The same holds across every sweep in $b$ (Figure~\ref{fig:exponent}b and, at $\nu=3$, Figure~\ref{fig:prime}): when $s$ changes at fixed $(\nu,p)$, the count moves the way $\Kstar$ predicts in $30$ of $36$ comparisons (about $38\%$ by chance), but only about half as far (post hoc). A predictor that ignores $s$ cannot pass this test, whereas matching $\Kstar$ to within one per run is already achieved by a constant in about half the runs.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/fig_spectra.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{From dense to sparse at $\nu=3$.} Fourier amplitudes $A_j/\max_kA_k$ of one converged product network per panel (chosen by rule: the lowest converged seed). Coloured bars pass the counting threshold (dotted, $0.2$); grey bars do not. Top: $b=\nu=3$, where Corollary~\ref{cor:dense} predicts every frequency with equal amplitude. Middle and bottom: $b=2.4$ and $b=2$.}

At the dense end, $b=\nu$ (Figure~\ref{fig:spectra}), every converged network at $\nu=3$ and $\nu=4$ uses every frequency, most with near-equal amplitudes, as Corollary~\ref{cor:dense} predicts; density alone is not diagnostic, since the network at initialization is already dense, but flatness is. At $\nu=5$ the picture is mixed.

\subsection{Moving the degree and the norm separately}

Theorem~\ref{thm:cyclic} says that $\nu$ and $b$ enter only through $s$. Our sweeps probe this only weakly. Under $L_2$ weight decay, raising the degree lowers the count, as Table~\ref{tab:kstar} predicts, in $5$ of $7$ comparisons; at fixed $s$, the count varies across degrees almost as much as it varies across $s$. At a few seeds per cell these tests are inconclusive (post hoc).

\begin{figure}[h!]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/fig_prime.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{The count across primes}, product networks at $\nu=3$. Lines: $\Kstar$ (solid where exact, $p\le37$; dashed where best found, Appendix~\ref{app:exp}). Markers: median over converged seeds, with the range. $\Kstar$ is not monotone in $p$ (at $s=1.25$ it is $9,7,6$ at $p=29,31,37$); the trained counts do not follow these steps.}
\label{fig:prime}
\end{figure}

Across primes (Figure~\ref{fig:prime}), the count stays within one of $\Kstar$ in most $(p,s)$ cells ($20$ of $28$, against $16$ for the best constant; post hoc), but it does not follow the non-monotone steps of $\Kstar$.
