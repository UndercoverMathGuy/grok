# R20-cyclic-theorem — literature check (2026-09-25)

Item: cyclic.tex thm:cyclic (+ prop:block, app:thm). The claim: the max L_{2,b} margin over interference-free product networks equals nu^{-(nu-b)/b} B_p Gamma_{nu/b}(p). For b<nu, every optimum has amplitudes proportional to a maximiser, uses |spt y*| frequencies, and puts each frequency on exactly nu equal neurons with orthogonal chain vectors.

## Verdict: PARTIALLY_KNOWN
- **The theorem itself (value, sparsity, necessity of exactly nu neurons, the nu-b pricing): NOT FOUND.** No paper computes a max-margin value for b<nu. None derives a frequency count from a budget programme, and none proves that exactly nu neurons per frequency is *norm-optimal* (necessary at equality).
- **The attaining construction (Prop. block): KNOWN.** Marchetti, Kunin, Myers, Acosta, Miolane, "Sequential Group Composition", ICML 2026 (arXiv 2602.03655), App. B, **Lemma B.8 Case 1**. There, N >= (k+1) n_rho^{k+1} sigma-pi-sigma (= our product) neurons solve the per-irrep cost minimisation. The inputs are multiplied by the unitary scalar e^{pi i j/(k+1)} and the output by e^{-pi i j k/(k+1)}/(k+1), for j=1..k+1. Real irreps take the real parts. Cross terms are then scaled by (1/(k+1)) sum_j e^{-2 pi i j (k-|A|)/(k+1)}, and this vanishes "since A is a proper subset... k-|A| != 0 mod k+1" (their eq. 71). For cyclic groups (n_rho=1) this is k+1 = nu neurons per frequency with phases pi l/nu on inputs and n*alpha_l on the output. **That is our nu-block exactly** (same rotated roots of unity, same cancellation argument). They use it only as a sufficient construction for square-loss minimisation along AGF dynamics, not as a norm/margin optimum. They also have no necessity statement and no count.

## Other prior art checked
- **Morwani et al. ICLR 2024** (arXiv 2311.07568, fetched): the b=nu=3 endpoint (Thm 7, dense, every frequency used). Our theorem at b=nu reduces to it; already cited.
- **Li, Liang, Shi, Song, Zhou, AISTATS 2025** (arXiv 2402.09469 / PMLR 258, fetched): the L_{2,k+1} max margin for k-input power neurons with m >= 2^{2k-2}(p-1). Each neuron uses one frequency and every frequency is used. This is the b=nu endpoint for power neurons, not product neurons. Their width 2^{2k-2} per frequency, against our nu for product neurons, matches Marchetti's Waring factor 2^k (Lemma B.6/Remark B.7: a square-free monomial needs >= 2^{k-1} powers, citing Carlini et al. 2011). Already cited.
- **Gromov 2023** (arXiv 2301.02679, PDF read): analytic quadratic-net solution. It uses all (p-1)/2 frequencies, random phases under the constraint phi1+phi2=phi3 (Eq. 12), and cancels cross terms only approximately (Eq. 15, large N). No norm or margin optimality. It explicitly says width adds redundancy, not features. RELATED only; our block makes the cancellation exact with nu neurons.
- **Tian, "Composing Global Solutions to Reasoning Tasks via Algebraic Objects in Neural Nets", NeurIPS 2025** (arXiv 2410.01779 v4, PDF read). Quadratic nets, L2 loss, weight decay. **Corollary 2**: a global solution with "each frequency ... affiliated with 6 hidden nodes (order-6)", built from 3rd/4th roots of unity (Table 1). **Corollary 4/5**: order-4 and mixed order-4/6 solutions. **Theorem 5** ("Occam's Razer"): "lower-order solutions are preferred if trained with L2 regularization". These global solutions cover all frequencies (L2 loss, not margin). The count of neurons per frequency here is a property of the construction, not a norm optimum. Closest *dynamics/weight-decay* precedent for "fixed neurons per frequency via roots of unity".
- **Kam-Bessafi-Cadet 2609.17926** (from the sweep): convex l2 amplitude budget, which gives dense. Covered in the sweep item.
- Huang & Li 2511.23443 (sine MLP expressivity/generalisation): not relevant.
- No work found that prices sparsity by nu-b (a margin that depends on the exponent gap). The generic "homogeneity/potential exponent decides sparse vs dense" idea is Jacobs-Montufar 2605.19458 (sweep).
