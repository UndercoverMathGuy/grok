### [medium] Uncovering a Universal Abstract Algorithm for Modular Addition in Neural Networks (McCracken et al.)
https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.18266

**What they show.** Much less than the searcher's rating implies. Theorem 4.7 is a SUFFICIENCY bound over frequencies 'drawn uniformly at random from {1,...,n/2}': if m > (2 ln n - 2 ln(2-2rho))/(ln(pi/delta) - 1) then w.p. rho the logit gap exceeds delta*m. Corollary 4.8: O(log n) frequencies SUFFICE for margin Omega(log n). There is no necessity direction, no optimisation over K, no regulariser and no norm — their weight decay is a fixed 1e-4 L2 hyperparameter, never an object of analysis. Critically they REAFFIRM the dense max-margin: 'Analogously to Morwani et al. [7], it gives that the maximum margin solution requires all frequencies to be learned.' Their sparse/dense dial is architectural: one-hot 1-layer learns floor(n/2) types, and 'adding either depth, or a trainable embedding matrix for inputs, causes the network to transition from learning floor(n/2) types of neurons to much fewer types.' And they explicitly disclaim the mechanism: 'While we are the first to point out how to cause a phase transition from O(n) to O(log(n)) learned frequencies (Fig. 4), we don't know why it occurs. Theorem 4.7 only says this solution should have great margins, and it does.' The MAIS agenda independently scopes them: 'A heuristic of McCracken et al. ... suggests O(log p) features suffice for deeper networks; whether trained shallow networks track any such rate is open.'

**What remains ours.** The mechanism, which they name as an open question. C1's derivation of the count from the regulariser price, the argmax K* = argmax M_K/K^{nu/q}, and the coverage functional M_K are untouched. Decisively: their dial is depth/trainable-embeddings, ours is q at FIXED architecture — and since adding a trainable embedding or a layer raises nu from 2 to 3, their Fig 4 transition is a special case our functional explains. We should claim that explicitly. Their O(log n) is sufficiency for randomly chosen frequencies; ours is the optimum over chosen K-sets, and only ours predicts the count CHANGES at fixed p and fixed architecture when q moves. Ours also predicts a nu-dependent constant; theirs has none.

**Required action.** cite / differentiate in intro. Frame as the paper that poses our question and state that we answer their stated open problem ('we don't know why it occurs'). Do NOT claim first observation of the dense-to-sparse transition or of a logarithmic count-vs-p law — both are theirs; concede priority in one sentence. Must add the decisive separating experiment prominently: hold architecture fixed and move only q, which their depth/embedding account cannot produce. Also state that our nu accounting explains their Fig 4 (depth and trainable embeddings both raise nu), turning a rival into a corollary.

### [medium] Fourier Circuits in Neural Networks and Transformers: Modular Arithmetic with Multiple Inputs (Li, Liang, Shi, Song, Zhou)
https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.09469

**What they show.** Verified by direct read of Eq (2): their network is phi(theta_i, a) = (u_{i,1}(a_1) + ... + u_{i,k}(a_k))^k * w_i — literally our C3 'x^n activation on n-operand sums' exact family, with nu = k+1. Their constraint set is Theta := {||theta||_{2,k+1} <= 1} and their regularised objective is L_lambda(theta) = mean CE + lambda*||theta||_{2,k+1}, i.e. q = nu = k+1 exactly, everywhere. Theorem 4.1: for m >= 2^{2k-1}(p-1)/2 the max L_{2,k+1}-margin is gamma* = 2(k!)/((2k+2)^{(k+1)/2}(p-1)p^{(k-1)/2}), each neuron is one-sparse in Fourier space, and 'For each frequency zeta in {1,...,(p-1)/2}, there exists one neuron using this frequency only.' Appendix sections F.1/F.2 are titled 'All Frequencies are Used' and 'All Frequencies are Used for General k Version'. A grep for any other exponent returns nothing: q < nu is never touched.

**What remains ours.** The entire q < nu side of the family, which is where all our content lives: the sparse counts, the K* formula, the measured ladder, and the q-sweep. Their contribution is one endpoint of our dial, at general nu. Our nsum results (nu=3 K*=5 lambda-stable, nu=4 median 6, the q=nu-to-l2 recipe) are all outside their theorem. But the ARCHITECTURE and the general-nu dense endpoint are theirs, not ours and not Morwani's.

**Required action.** cite / reframe claim. Two concrete edits. (1) C2 currently says 'Morwani et al. proved ALL frequencies are used, but only for L_{2,nu}' — the general-nu version of that endpoint is Li et al.'s, so the sentence must read 'Morwani et al. at nu=3, extended to general nu by Li et al.' Attributing the general-nu endpoint to Morwani alone is an error a reviewer will catch and will read as careless scholarship. (2) C3 must not present the n-operand x^n family as a new construction; restate its novelty as the q-dial on a family Li et al. introduced and solved at q=nu. Framing our exact families as 'new' when the nu=k+1 family is published with a closed-form margin is the single most likely source of a novelty objection on C3.

### [medium] Survival of the Fittest Representation: A Case Study with Modular Addition (Ding, Guo, Michaud, Liu, Tegmark)

**Required action.** cite / differentiate in intro — plus a REQUIRED experiment. ### [low] Neural Networks Provably Learn Spectral Representations for Group Composition (He, Wang, Zhang, Chen, Yang)
https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02993

**What they show.** Less overlap than rated once the setting is read. Training is projected gradient flow on the unit sphere with the scaling factors a_m HELD FIXED in Stage I, under a Taylor-expanded approximate risk ('we apply the Taylor expansion log(sum exp(s_j)) ~ log n + ... to the cross-entropy loss'). There is no weight decay anywhere, no penalty exponent, no norm to price with — the norm is pinned by the sphere constraint — and no optimisation over how many irreps to use. The non-abelian theorem is per-NEURON: 'each neuron m selects exactly one' irrep, with rank-one cross-layer alignment; they never predict a network-level count. The abelian result (Thm 5.1) is the dense one: the learned representation is 'uniformly distributed over the set of non-trivial irreducible representations', driven by a lottery ('the initially dominant representation wins almost surely, while all competing representations are exponentially suppressed').

**What remains ours.** The entire pricing apparatus and every count. Their per-neuron single-irrep result is Chughtai-flavoured and compatible with ours; our S5 claim is a NETWORK-level count (one faithful irrep plus sign patch, 32/32), which their framework does not address and which a naive extension of their abelian diversification would get wrong. Their abelian uniform diversification is a zero-weight-decay, fixed-radial-scale, surrogate-risk statement — a different ensemble from the regularised max-margin endpoint we characterise — which is exactly the scope limit the MAIS agenda records: 'Their comparison with exact cross-entropy is finite-horizon, so it does not transfer the asymptotic theorem.' Their lottery mechanism also agrees with our own finding that identity is a coverage-weighted seed lottery.

**Required action.** cite / differentiate in intro. One paragraph naming the three scope limits (no weight decay, radial scales frozen, Taylor-surrogate risk) and stating that our claim concerns the regularised endpoint, not their flow. Position C4's Z_120 divisor-frequency prediction explicitly against their abelian uniform-diversification, since that is the one place the two predictions visibly diverge — and say which ensemble each governs, so the divergence reads as scope rather than conflict. Do not let this paper go uncited in a group-composition submission; it is the most recent group-side theory and reviewers will know it.

**Required action.** cite / differentiate in intro.
